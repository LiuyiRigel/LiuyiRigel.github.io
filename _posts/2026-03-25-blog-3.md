## 2026/3/27
这两天花了些时间重新看了看自己的研究方向

之前对时间序列的处理总是想着转成图像信号之后再用视觉模型做处理，陷入经验主义的怪圈了。之前的人怎么做，那我也想着往这个方向试试。

回过头来想想现在科研真的很难，自己也不太觉得是在做科研，看文献的时候，简单的文章看不上，高效有用的文章自己有做不到。眼高手低，能力不足反而是大多数时间的状态。发现了一个方法之后，做了些尝试，但效果不佳，可解释性也很差，像是缝合出来的怪东西。

时序处理，特别是做声发射/ 工业故障方向的时频分析完全的做创新很难。前人做的数学建模用了多少年，到现在也还在用，基础创新做不到，就只能做些方法创新，A+B，B+C，AI大方向下，大家都在All in，一个方法出来了，细分的垂直领域迅速就会涌现出自己的工程实现。

这两天回归到原始信号的处理，找了些相关的时序模型复现试试，至少在模型的可解释性上做些提升吧。能力不足，就多些尝试吧，但愿时间足够。

今天把之前过年期间的照片处理了一点。

## 2026/10/8

### To ApTem 

Aptem, I really don’t know how to thank you. Let’s put my situation aside for now and talk about you. I recently heard about a plague outbreak and cases of unexplained pneumonia in Moscow, which reminded me of that terrifying winter in 2019. Please, Aptem, make sure to take good care of yourself—stay healthy and boost your immunity. And of course, take care of your little brother too. I hope everything goes well for you....

Now, let’s talk about me. After hearing what you said, I’ve done a lot of soul-searching and talked it over with my real-life friends. At the time, I was crying so hard I could barely move—my hands and feet were shaking, and I even developed respiratory alkalosis—but thankfully, I pulled through.

I gradually realized that the reason I was in so much pain was that I was trying too hard to live. I desperately wanted to succeed and desperately wanted to live up to everyone’s expectations, but of course, this caused me extreme pain and torment....

As you said, aptem, I should allow myself to fail. Do you remember when we first met? Back then, I said I’d found a way to combat the void: by constantly keeping myself busy—that would keep me occupied and leave me no time to dwell on my inner thoughts and feelings. So that’s what I did. I attended academic conferences, applied for grants, and constantly learned new skills and created new things. That way, I could numb myself; I could escape my pain by filling myself up. But as the saying goes, “Either die in silence, or explode in silence.” After constantly accumulating this pain, one day I finally reached my limit. I could no longer suppress the anguish inside me, nor could I keep pretending to everyone that I was good enough and incredibly strong.

I finally erupted, burning like a raging fire—from the inside out—until my muscles, my fat, and my bones were ultimately consumed...

I fought desperately to stay alive because I knew I was afraid of death, afraid of being abandoned, and afraid that others would despise me for the weakness and pain I showed. So I struggled with all my might to become that “perfect person.”

Shortly after talking with you, I made an appointment with the school counselor to see if my distress and anxiety could be alleviated. I told him that I was living alone in a rented apartment near campus and had spent the entire summer studying and preparing for exams, but I had no social life—just studying, working out, studying, and working out. That was my life. I simplified everything to the bare minimum: boiled chicken breast for protein, boiled broccoli for dietary fiber and vitamins, and rice for carbohydrates—not a single drop of seasoning or oil. I ate like this for a month. I cut off almost all social contact. I treated myself like a machine—as long as I had the necessary intake, I could keep running...

But I’m not a machine; I’m a human being—someone who needs social interaction, warmth, and the company of others. As Marx said, “Man is the sum total of all his social relations.” I’m afraid by then, I could no longer be called a human being.

I’m always pondering what I truly want, but during this time, what I want most is a hug—the kind of warmth I felt as a child tucked under my mother’s blanket, cradled in her arms. That warmth and sense of security that let me let down all my defenses and stop pretending to be strong.

After receiving your message—which coincided with our country’s National Day—I realized I couldn’t go on living like this. I went home for a visit; it was the second time this year I’d seen my parents. I was finally able to truly rest...
Sometimes I really don’t know what to do, Aptem. I’m trying so hard to live, but I can’t see an end to the pain I’m feeling.

I always watch the news and see people suffering in this world—poverty, injustice, wickedness, and deceit. I’m afraid of becoming that kind of person; I want to stay kind forever.

I’ve saved your words in my notes, and whenever I think of them, I read them—it always gives me strength.
You're always so serious with me, Aptem. You're the second person in the world who's ever taken me this seriously.
